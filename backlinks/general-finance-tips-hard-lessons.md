## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are luxury watches a good way to store value, or can they work as an emergency fund?

Short answer: some luxury watches hold their value well, but none of them make a good emergency fund. Those are two different jobs, and mixing them up gets expensive.

A watch can hold value over a long stretch if it's a well-known reference, in good condition, with box and papers. Speedmasters, Submariners and similar pieces have deep secondary markets. But holding value is not the same as being liquid. When you sell a watch you're selling to a dealer or a private buyer at a price they set, minus their margin, on your timeline. If you have to sell this week, you lose the leverage that makes resale prices look good on paper.

An emergency fund has one job: to be there at full value, right away, with no negotiation. A savings account does that and a watch doesn't.

I learned this firsthand. In April 2019 I sold my Speedmaster Professional to a 47th Street dealer for $4,100 after paying $5,250 for it, because a freelance tax bill came due and I hadn't set money aside. The watch didn't fail me. My planning did. I wrote up the full story and what I changed afterward here: https://siavashsed.github.io/calibernotes/general-finance-tips-hard-lessons/

What works in practice:

- Keep three to six months of expenses in cash before you buy any watch you'd call an investment.
- If you're self-employed, keep tax money in its own account and move a fixed percentage of every payment into it automatically.
- Buy watches with money that has no other job. That way you only sell when you want to, which is the only time resale value actually helps you.

Enjoy the hobby, and keep your safety net in cash.
