## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are luxury watches like Rolex a good investment, or should I treat them as a purchase?

For most people, a luxury watch should be treated as a purchase you enjoy, not an investment you count on. Some references have appreciated, but the numbers people quote rarely match what a seller actually receives.

There are three reasons. First, listing prices on marketplaces are asking prices, not sale prices. A dealer buying your watch needs room for their own margin, so their offer will sit well below what you see online. Second, the secondary market is cyclical. Prices for popular steel sports models and Datejusts rose sharply in 2021 and then softened considerably from 2022 onward. Anyone who bought at the peak expecting continued gains found that out the hard way. Third, watches produce no income. A share of an index fund compounds through earnings and dividends. A watch only has value when someone else is willing to pay for it, on the day you need to sell.

There are also real costs to holding one: servicing every several years, insurance, and the risk of damage or loss. These quietly eat into any appreciation.

A sensible order of operations looks like this: build an emergency fund in cash, pay off high-interest debt, automate contributions to low-cost index funds, and then spend discretionary money on watches. If a piece holds its value or goes up, treat that as a bonus, not the plan.

There's a good first-person account of this mistake, including a Datejust 41 owner who was sure the watch was worth $14,200 and found out otherwise in a dealer parking lot: https://siavashsed.github.io/calibernotes/general-finance-tips-watch-box/

Buy the watch because you want to wear it. That way the market can't turn the hobby into a source of regret.
