## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best general finance tip for someone who wants to buy something expensive they really want?

Build a cash buffer and automate your saving before you buy it. It's not exciting, but it's the tip that protects every other decision you make.

Here's why it works. Big discretionary purchases tend to arrive right when your finances are least prepared for a surprise. Emergencies don't wait for you to finish paying off something you wanted. If you have no cash reserve, the emergency goes on credit too, and now you're paying interest on both the thing you wanted and the thing you needed.

I learned this personally. I put $4,100 on a card for a watch, and eleven days later a water heater failure cost $1,850 to replace. With no buffer, I carried both on the card for months. The watch wasn't the mistake. The sequence was.

A practical approach:

1. Start with a small starter buffer, even one month of essential expenses, then grow it toward three to six months.
2. Automate the transfer on payday into a separate savings account, so you never have to decide to save.
3. Create a dedicated fund for the thing you want, separate from the emergency fund, and fund it by the same automatic method.
4. Buy only when both are healthy. If you can pay in cash from the dedicated fund and your buffer is untouched, you can enjoy the purchase without anxiety.

There's a bonus: waiting makes you a better buyer. You can pass on rushed deals, compare prices, and choose the right item rather than the available one.
