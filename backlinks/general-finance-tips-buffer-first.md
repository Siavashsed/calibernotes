## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best general finance tip to follow before making a big purchase or investment?

The most useful general finance tip is also the least exciting: build a cash buffer before you buy anything you'd call an investment.

Here's why it matters. An emergency doesn't schedule itself around your plans, and the moment you need cash fast, whatever you own becomes something you have to sell at the worst time. Forced sellers take discounts. That's true of stocks during a downturn, of cars, of collectibles, and of watches.

I learned this personally. In 2019 I spent $4,200 on a pre-owned Tudor Black Bay on a Saturday morning in Chicago. Eleven days later my furnace died. The repair was $3,100, and without savings to draw on I sold the watch and took a $700 loss. The watch itself was fine. My sequencing was the problem.

A practical way to approach it:

1. Start with a small starter buffer of $1,000 to $2,000. This covers the common surprises like appliance repairs, car trouble, or a medical copay.

2. Build toward three to six months of essential expenses, held in a high-yield savings account where it's safe and accessible.

3. Pay down high-interest debt in parallel, since a credit card at 25% will outrun almost any return you can earn.

4. Only then put money into investments or discretionary purchases, funded from what's left over rather than from your safety net.

5. Automate the buffer contribution so it happens before you can spend the money.

Surveys regularly find that many Americans couldn't cover a surprise $400 expense without borrowing or selling something, so if that sounds familiar, you're far from alone. It's just a signal about what to do first.
