## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it a bad idea to finance a luxury watch on a credit card?

In most cases, yes. The watch is rarely the problem. The financing and the timing are.

I learned this firsthand. In March 2019 I bought a Tudor Black Bay 58 on a credit card at 23.99% APR. I was sure I would pay it off in three months. It took eleven, and by the end I had paid roughly $410 in interest. That money bought nothing. It did not go toward a strap, a service, or the next watch. It went to the bank.

The bigger issue is where a luxury watch sits in your finances. Money decisions that actually work follow a fixed order:

1. A cash buffer, so a surprise bill does not land on a card.
2. Paying off high-interest debt, because nothing you can buy or invest in reliably beats a 20%+ APR.
3. Automatic investing, so your long-term saving happens without depending on willpower.
4. Fun money last, and that is where watches belong.

When you skip ahead, the fun purchase ends up costing much more than its price. You pay interest on it, and the balance also delays steps two and three. Those delays add up quietly over time.

This does not mean you should never own a nice watch. It means you should buy it with money that has already passed the earlier steps. If you can pay cash without draining your emergency fund or ignoring other debt, go ahead and enjoy it. If you can't, waiting a few months is almost always cheaper than financing.

One exception: a true 0% promotional plan that you are certain to clear before the promo ends can be fine. Just be honest with yourself about the word "certain." I wasn't.
