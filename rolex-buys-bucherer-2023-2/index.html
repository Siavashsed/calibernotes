Rolex Just Bought Its Biggest Retailer, and Your Waitlist Changed Forever

Rolex buying Bucherer in August 2023 was the biggest watch retail story in decades. Here's what the deal means for buyers, dealers and pre-owned prices.

On August 24, 2023, Rolex announced it was buying Bucherer, the Lucerne retailer that sells more Rolex watches than anyone else on the planet, and the brand now controls the counter where you'll be told whether you get a Submariner. I found out from a text from a dealer friend at 7:40 in the morning, three words long: "Did you see?" Two years earlier I'd spent an awkward 25 minutes at the Tourneau Bucherer on 57th Street in Manhattan trying to sound casual about a GMT-Master II while the sales associate scrolled through my purchase history on an iPad. My position is simple. This deal is good for Rolex, mostly neutral for the wealthy client with history, and quietly bad for everyone else.

The numbers explain the move. Morgan Stanley and LuxeConsult estimated Rolex's 2022 sales at around CHF 9.3 billion, close to 30% of the Swiss watch market, in their annual Morgan Stanley Swiss watch industry report. Meanwhile the Federation of the Swiss Watch Industry recorded record exports of roughly CHF 24.8 billion for 2022. A brand that big, in a market that hot, buying its own biggest storefront... that isn't a defensive move. It's a land grab.

Why an 87-Year-Old Owner Made the Rolex Bucherer Deal Inevitable

Jörg G. Bucherer ran the company his grandfather founded in 1888, and he had no direct heirs. Bucherer runs around 100 stores across Europe, the UK and the US, the American footprint coming mostly from its 2018 purchase of Tourneau. Rolex reportedly makes up close to half of Bucherer's watch revenue. If that business had landed with a private equity fund or a rival group like Richemont or LVMH, Rolex would have handed its biggest sales channel to someone else's strategy.

So Rolex bought it. The official line from Rolex is that Bucherer keeps its name, its staff and its independence, and keeps selling other brands. I'd take that at face value for now. Rolex is famously patient, and gutting a 135-year-old name would be clumsy in a way Geneva rarely is.

But independence on paper and independence in practice are different things. When your owner is also your most important supplier, every allocation conversation changes, even if nobody says a word about it.

What Other Authorized Dealers Are Saying Off the Record

I've spent the last week talking to people who run Rolex corners at independent jewelers in the US and the UK. Not one wanted to be named. Every one of them asked the same thing in slightly different words: am I now competing with the manufacturer? For more on this, see Rolex Just Bought Its Biggest Retailer, and the Trade Is Spooked.

It's a fair worry. Bucherer was already the first partner on the Rolex Certified Pre-Owned program, which launched in December 2022. That program means Rolex can now touch the primary sale, the resale and the service relationship through a store it owns. An independent AD in Ohio can't match that. And if the hot references get steered toward Bucherer windows first, smaller dealers will feel it in foot traffic long before they see it in any allocation spreadsheet.

The misconception worth killing

Plenty of collectors on forums assume this means shorter waitlists. I don't buy it. Rolex controls supply because scarcity is the product. Owning a retailer gives it better data on who buys what, and better data tends to make gatekeeping sharper, not looser.

The Grey Market Was Already Cooling Before the Announcement

Timing matters. The deal landed while secondary prices were sliding. Data from WatchCharts shows its Rolex market index sitting roughly a quarter below its spring 2022 peak by mid 2023. Flippers who paid huge premiums in 2021 are sitting on losses, and the Daytona that traded at nearly triple retail now gets offers closer to double.

When I tracked asking prices on a steel Explorer 124270 this spring, the premium over retail shrank from about $4,000 to under $1,500 in roughly five months. That's the window Rolex picked to own a pre-owned channel. Cooling prices make a manufacturer-backed, warrantied pre-owned watch look like the safe choice, and Rolex gets to set the tone for what "fair" resale looks like.

If you've been treating watches as a portfolio line, my piece on finance habits that build real wealth instead of the illusion of it is worth a read before you chase the next reference. For more on this, see Rolex Bought Bucherer and the Watch World Still Hasn't Processed It.

Where This Leaves You at the Counter

I'll admit some doubt here. I'm not 100% sure the client relationship changes much for the average buyer, at least not this year. My friend Daniel walked into a Bucherer in Lucerne the week after the news, half joking that he'd ask "to speak to the owner," and honestly the associate laughed, sold him a strap, and told him the Sea-Dweller list was "long." Same as always. Maybe the revolution is slower than the headlines. Maybe I'm reading too much into a press release.

Still, the structural picture is clear. Here's what I'd expect over the next two to three years:

  • Bucherer stores getting flagship-style Rolex boutiques and earlier access to new releases
  • Certified Pre-Owned spreading to more Bucherer doors, with Rolex-set pricing
  • Independent ADs leaning harder on Tudor, Omega and independents to keep margins
  • Purchase history shared more tightly across the network

This follows a pattern I wrote about after Watches & Wonders Geneva 2022 showed luxury watches had survived the pandemic. Brands came out of the crisis with more pricing power and less patience for middlemen. The digital pivot of the 2020 fair taught them they could talk to buyers directly. Bucherer is the physical version of that same lesson.

If you're on a Rolex waitlist at any authorized dealer, ask your salesperson this week, in plain words, whether their allocation has changed since August, and write down what they say. Then get a second list going at a different retailer group so one channel doesn't own your entire shot at the watch.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Rolex buy Bucherer?

Bucherer was Rolex's largest retailer and its owner, Jörg G. Bucherer, had no direct heirs. Buying it kept the biggest Rolex sales channel out of a rival's hands and gave Rolex direct control over retail and pre-owned sales.

Will Rolex owning Bucherer make waitlists shorter?

Probably not. Rolex controls supply on purpose, and owning a retailer gives it better client data, which tends to make allocation more selective rather than faster.

Does Bucherer still sell other watch brands after the Rolex deal?

Yes. Rolex said Bucherer would keep its name, staff and independent operations, including its multi-brand business, though many dealers expect Rolex's influence on the stores to grow over time.
