## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I build an emergency fund before buying an expensive hobby item like a luxury watch?

Yes, in almost every case, and the reason is mostly about interest and liquidity.

First, the math. If you finance a big purchase on a credit card, the cost is far higher than the sticker price. I once put $7,800 on a card at 24.99% APR and took eleven months to pay it off. The interest alone was roughly $900. That is a real cost for an item that does nothing to improve your financial position.

Second, luxury hobby items are poor emergency funds. Watches, cameras, instruments and similar goods are illiquid. When you need cash fast, you typically sell at a discount to what you paid, and the dealer or buyer knows you are under pressure. A cash cushion does not have that problem.

Here is the order I would follow:

1. Build a starter emergency fund of one month of expenses.
2. Pay off any debt above roughly 8 to 10 percent interest.
3. Grow the emergency fund to three to six months of essential costs.
4. Only then set up a dedicated, automated hobby fund and buy with cash.

The key idea is to remove decisions rather than rely on willpower. Automate the transfers so saving happens before you can spend, and avoid checking your balance obsessively, since that tends to trigger reactive choices.

A dedicated hobby fund also changes the experience. You can wait for the right piece at the right price, negotiate calmly, and enjoy the purchase without a monthly reminder of the debt behind it.

If you want a collector's perspective on this with more detail, I wrote up my own experience here: https://siavashsed.github.io/calibernotes/general-finance-tips-collectors/

The short version: protect your safety net first, and the hobby gets better, not smaller.
