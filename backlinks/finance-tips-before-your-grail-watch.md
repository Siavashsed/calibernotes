**Title:** I financed a steel sports watch on a credit card expecting it to appreciate. Here's what it actually cost me.

Long time lurker. Sharing this in case it saves someone from my mistake.

In March 2022 I put $11,400 on a credit card for a steel sports watch. I figured grey-market prices only went one direction and I could flip it by summer if I needed to. By August the grey price had dropped about $2,600, and I was carrying the balance at roughly 24% APR. So the watch was losing value and I was paying interest on that loss at the same time.

Now I check three things before any watch purchase. I need three to six months of expenses in cash. My retirement and index contributions have to be automated so they go out before I see the money. And the watch gets paid for outright from a separate fund I've saved up for it. If I can't tick all three, the purchase waits.

None of this is exciting, but it changed how I enjoy the hobby. A watch bought from surplus is fun to wear. A watch bought on a card reminds you of itself every month.

How do others here handle it? Do you keep a dedicated watch fund, or just buy when it feels right?

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I buy a luxury watch on a credit card if I expect it to hold its value?

Short answer: no. I say that as someone who did exactly this and paid for it.

What makes it tempting is the belief that the watch will hold or gain value. In early 2022, steel sports models from the major brands were trading well above retail on the grey market, and buying one felt more like investing than spending. In March 2022 I put $11,400 on a card for one, planning to sell at a profit by summer if I had to. By August the grey-market price had dropped about $2,600, and I was carrying the balance at roughly 24% APR. I was losing money on both sides at once.

A few things to understand before you do it:

1. Secondary watch prices are a market, not a floor. They move with sentiment, interest rates and how much supply the manufacturer releases. Nothing guarantees a model stays above retail.

2. Credit card interest is a guaranteed loss. Even a watch that holds its value perfectly can't outrun 20%+ APR for long.

3. Selling is harder than it looks. A quick sale usually means taking a dealer's offer well below the prices you see listed online.

Here is what I'd do instead. Build an emergency buffer of three to six months of expenses. Automate your retirement and investment contributions so they happen first. Then save for the watch in a dedicated fund and buy it outright. It takes longer, but you'll own it free and clear, and a dip in market value won't matter because you were never planning to sell.

I wrote up the full sequence, including the mistakes I made, here: https://siavashsed.github.io/calibernotes/finance-tips-before-your-grail-watch/

Buy the watch you love. Just pay for it with money that has already done its other jobs.
